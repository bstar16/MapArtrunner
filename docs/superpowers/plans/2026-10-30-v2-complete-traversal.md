## Decisions settled by current source

### Revised whole-run allowance and independent movement watchdog

**Changed ruling:** the earlier preserve-global1200 instruction is withdrawn for arbitrary Task8 builds. It was a milestone-sized production constant, not a supported-map-size contract. ChildTask7 source/evidence is not retroactively changed. A plan-size-derived finite allowance replaces it; a separate stationary-movement watchdog prevents the larger allowance from hiding a stuck player.

Compute once from the immutable plan and settings before allocating canonical lanes. Let `N=target count`, `L=canonical lane count`, and `D=L*(width-1)` be total full-forward X walking distance. Derive L using the exact LanePlanner center formula (first/last center and ceiling division by stride), including depth1..5 and clipped last lanes. Use checked long arithmetic throughout. The chosen allowance is:

`runLimit = max(1200, 3*N + 2*D*8 + (L+1)*52 + 2*(L-1)*120 + 80)`.

Terms are: up to three issuance callbacks per target; both complete physical passes at an admitted overall allowance of eight active callbacks per X block; two initial entries plus at most L-1 extra X-transit entries; both sets of complete adjacent transitions; and final VERIFY80. Entry/centering at each subsequent Z checkpoint or selected destination is already inside its hop120. The selective route below has at most the X distance of a complete reverse pass: each needed corrective X leg substitutes for at least one skipped canonical X lane, rather than adding another whole pass. This is an overall work allowance, **not** a promise that every target may consume its maximum pending/retry delay serially; delays can exhaust the finite allowance even while individual local limits remain valid. Eight callbacks/block is an explicit provisional pacing allowance (2.5 blocks/second at20 callbacks/second), not a claimed measured native speed. Native scale acceptance below must validate it; change that single derivation in a reviewed correction if real healthy progress disproves it, never silently raise a fixture timeout.

Examples: full12x10 yields N120/L2/D22 and raw1188, therefore retains1200; full128x128 yields N16384/L26/D3302 and109468. Huge source dimensions do not wrap the limit or become small positive deadlines. Reject an unsupported/overflowing calculation before lane allocation with UNSUPPORTED_PLAN and all IDs retained. Bound the admitted run allowance to `Integer.MAX_VALUE` active callbacks, the chosen finite work-index domain, rather than saturating to Long.MAX_VALUE; this accepts ordinary map sizes and explicitly refuses extreme work requests. Retain the importer's existing tracked100MiB NBT/depth and dense-int-volume validation. Sparse source dimensions still count full physical route length; do not size work from target count alone. A representable allowance is not a claim that every enormous input is operationally practical; no new loader or memory-admission framework is introduced.

Keep `eligibleTicks` monotone for the entire run and compare it to this frozen allowance. Placements, observations, re-confirmations, clipping, new lanes, pass changes and pause/resume cannot increase the allowance. Preserve current successful-later-final-scan precedence at the exact deadline. Small native variants may retain their stricter1200 fixture deadline even when their production allowance is larger; report those two bounds distinctly.

Add one owner-local movement watchdog: after entering WALK in Substage.LANE or Substage.TRANSIT, track the credited high-water longitudinal coordinate in its actual direction and supported-WALK decisions since a0.25-block gain beyond that coordinate. On the40th supported-WALK decision without that cumulative gain, return `walkStalled` and fail WALK_NO_PROGRESS with released controls and retained IDs. Count only actual policy WALKING decisions (support ready, yaw aligned) in those two substages; adjacent SHIFT keeps its existing120 gate rather than acquiring an earlier stall failure. HOLDING_SUPPORT/HOLDING_YAW retain but do not reset the counter, preserving their existing deadline/cause. Missing player and pause likewise cannot reset it. Sideways movement, oscillation and placement confirmations earn no movement credit. Rebase only at a genuinely entered next physical lane or the one forward-to-reverse orientation change; do not rebase on entry-stage jitter or schedule rechecking. This is one counter/coordinate pair in the traversal owner, not a scheduler or generic progress framework.

### Initial selection and target retention

Keep the traversal object created in `BuildEngine.start`; its initial provisional lane exposes the whole ExpectedFloor, which current `routeObservations` and feedback correlation already use. Select once on the first eligible callback containing a player, after observations are routed, before the first policy decision. Missing-player callbacks do not choose a lane; the frozen whole-run allowance still advances.

The engine supplies `List<Target> traversalTargets(Map<Integer, PlacementObservation> current)`: the plan-order union of ledger-unfinished targets and targets without a matching current accepted OBSERVED sample. This is an ephemeral selection view, not a second ledger. In particular, an observation missing on the selection callback cannot inherit a prior callback's confirmation and disappear. Placement/retry accounting continues using the existing ledger. At later lane-selection boundaries the same view makes absent current evidence conservative.

Selection rules: if every target is unfinished, use canonical lane 0/start (fresh build). Otherwise generate each useful lane's canonical start and earliest-unfinished progress candidate, preserving canonical direction/corridor/floor/id. Distance is ordinary Euclidean distance to the standing center `(x+.5, planeY+1, z+.5)`, with the v1 16.0-block tie rule and iteration order; prefer earlier lane and, within a lane's tie, the partial-progress candidate. Do not replace 16 with a squared-distance tolerance. Clip only the initial entry's start progress; keep its canonical end. A completely correct initial current scan enters VERIFY without movement, then needs a later scan. A useful target with no covering lane yields explicit INVARIANT_FAILURE, not empty-selection success.

Forward continuation visits canonical lanes from the selected index through the last index in order, even if later lanes are currently clean. All earlier IDs and targets behind initial progress remain in ledger truth for reverse selection. Ordinary same-context `resume(Context)` preserves the current owner and counters; it does not rerun smart selection. Out-of-corridor/below-plane entry retains COARSE_TRAVEL_UNAVAILABLE until the later coarse-travel task.

### Reverse selection and a finite physical route

Choose **adjacent Z checkpoints plus only-needed X transit**, replacing the earlier draft's full-width traversal of every clean reverse lane. On a128-wide plan, crossing20 clean lanes would add2540 X blocks; the chosen route needs at most one127-block X correction plus roughly100 Z blocks for that relocation. Selection still owns one fixed target entry at a time inside ForwardTraversal; no new scheduler or navigation framework.

At forward exhaustion, consult the current target view. With none unfinished, enter VERIFY. Otherwise select useful canonical lanes in descending original index, swapping each selected lane's original endpoints and reversing its direction. Freeze its full chosen entry while traveling. If source endpoint X differs from chosen start X, create one cardinal X leg at the current centerline as `Substage.TRANSIT` / SHIFT. Enter it with the existing52 bound, walk with the new no-progress watchdog and frozen global allowance, then begin the first Z hop. If X already matches, omit this leg. Advance Z through successive original centerlines at that X without traversing their widths or entering BUILD. Recheck usefulness at the selected entry, so an overlap repair can skip it and select a strictly lower index; never retarget arbitrarily mid-hop or restart a higher reverse index. End the single reverse pass when no unfinished target is coverable at or below the cursor, then VERIFY exposes any remaining/reopened work.

For E/W/E with only lane1 unfinished: correct X WEST once at lane2's centerline, make the NORTH hop, then enter reversed lane1 EAST at its west X and full Z. For last3->selected1, source/selected X already match: make the two adjacent NORTH hops and no X leg. For selected3->selected1, correct X once at lane3's centerline, then pass clean centerline2 without walking its width. Use actual coordinates, not parity alone. Both full passes conservatively bound the work allowance; real routing is usually shorter.

**What completes a hop:** start its120 clock after any needed X WALK has completed, upon ENDPOINT at the chosen X/current Z. That same120 includes centering this source, entering/walking the adjacent Z segment, and NEXT_ENTRY at the destination. WALK COMPLETE (within one longitudinal block) is insufficient. Complete only on NEXT_ENTRY READY_TO_WALK with both destination coordinates within inclusive ±0.25 of block centers and two aligned-yaw samples. An intermediate clean checkpoint uses a zero-length NORTH lane for that entry; the final chosen destination uses its real reversed EAST/WEST entry. Original centerlines are distinct and monotonically decrease. Only after that actual destination progress may the next hop start at zero. No X leg is inserted into an already-running hop, and changing checkpoint/selection never resets a still-live120 clock.

TRANSIT's X WALK uses the same movement policy, with sprint disabled, entry40/52/yaw bounds and retained support counters. Its end is centered by the following hop's ENDPOINT stage. It uses the frozen global allowance and supported-WALK watchdog, not a renewed120 clock. Preserve original floor/corridor ownership for X movement and the existing clipped corridor for each Z hop. At most L selected reverse lanes, L-1 adjacent reverse hops and L-1 corrective X legs are admitted; canceled-on-arrival selections still consume monotonically descending indexes. A stalled X leg fails WALK_NO_PROGRESS; a stalled hop retains its existing movement/transition failure. No checkpoint or completed placement can mint more whole-run time.

The engine maps TRANSIT to SHIFT and can retain existing support/opportunistic placement ordering. Repairs during transit remain real ledger work; re-evaluate selection at arrival. Do not silently call TRANSIT BUILD or claim clean lanes were never physically crossed.
